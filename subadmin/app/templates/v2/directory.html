What holding someone back does - and does not - change

A rule here changes only what this sync pushes to phones. Nothing in Active Directory, Entra or Exchange is touched - the person keeps their numbers in AD, stays in Outlook's address book, and can still be looked up by anyone with a mailbox. It is the difference between "not in the company phone book on 250 iPhones" and "hidden from the company".

Leave them out removes their card from every recipient on the next sync. Without their mobile keeps the card (name, email, title, desk phone) and drops the mobile number; phones that already hold the card get it rewritten. Without any phone numbers keeps name, email and title only. A rule for someone who is not being published anyway (disabled, unlicensed, hidden in Exchange) is kept and takes effect if that ever changes.

Only people with a mobile number is a directory-wide switch, not a list of names: every sync re-checks Entra, holds back anyone with no mobile number, and publishes them again the moment a number is added in AD. A person's own rule outranks it - "publish them without their mobile" still publishes them.

"Not published" shows the accounts the sync filtered out and why: disabled, guest, unlicensed (shared mailboxes, rooms, service accounts), hidden from the Exchange address book, or matching the service-account pattern. Those rules live in the sync's own configuration, not on this page.

How publishing works
The roster is read from Microsoft Graph, the rules above are applied, and each person becomes a stamped contact in every recipient's Exchange Contacts folder - iPhones sync that natively, so inbound calls show a name. Only contacts carrying the sync's stamp are ever read, changed or removed; personal contacts are never touched. Recipients must be in the Exchange fence group or Graph refuses the write. Only one sync runs at a time - a second "Sync now" is refused rather than run twice. The CardDAV book on directory.{{ base_domain }} is the same roster on a parked path for devices that can reach it.
