Approver Quick Start Guide

How to review, decide and administer access requests in the Approver Console. Everything described here is what the console does today — each screen is covered in the order you meet it.

🗺️ What This Console Is

One screen per approval step, and one decision per request.

Requesters submit forms on the main portal. Each request type carries a fixed chain of approval steps. When a request reaches a step, the person or address configured for that step is emailed and the request appears here, waiting. You approve, reject or return it; the portal moves it to the next step or closes it, emails the people who need to know, and writes every event to an audit trail.

👥

A role is a step

Dept. Manager, InfoSec, Human Resources, Legal, Chief Legal Officer, CTO, CEO. You see the requests that pass through your step.

➡️

Strictly in order

Only the current step can decide. Later steps can watch a request but cannot act early.

🔒

Deny by default

Every guard is enforced by the portal service, not the browser. An unassigned step has no approvers at all.

ℹ️
You never decide on your own request. If you submit a request that routes through your own step, the console shows it read-only and the service refuses the decision — separation of duties.

🔑 Signing In

Single sign-on — there is no separate portal password.
1

Open the Approver Portal

accessportal.nrpi.local/approver.html

Use the Approver Portal button in the top-right of the main portal, or go to the address above. Your CorpIT sign-in identifies you; the portal reads your name, email and group membership from it.

✔️

Because identity comes from sign-on, your decisions are recorded against your own name in the audit trail. Never review on someone else’s behalf from their session.

⚠️
“No approval access was found for your account.” You are signed in correctly, but you hold no approval rights yet. Ask IT to add you to the step’s approver group, or to grant you the step under Approval Access. Being a department manager is different — see Who May Approve What.
📘
Approver Guide and Requester portal sit next to Sign out in the top bar on every screen. Sign out ends your portal session; it does not sign you out of other CorpIT pages.

🎯 Choosing Your Role

The first screen after sign-in. One tile per step you are allowed to see.

Pick the step you are reviewing as. The tile you choose scopes everything that follows: the dashboard counts, the queues and the sidebar all describe that step. Use Switch role in the top bar to come back.

A

What each tile tells you

Step name and description

The display name an administrator set for the step, and what that step reviews. Chief Legal Officer is a step of its own (Out-of-Country Access only), separate from the Legal team's step on other forms.

Blue badge — “N request types”

How many of the six request types route through this step. InfoSec sees all six; the CTO and CEO steps see one.

Red badge — “Named as manager on N requests”

Appears on the Dept. Manager tile. It counts the requests that name your address as the requester’s manager. Those are the only requests that tile lets you act on.

Team acknowledgments tile

Appears when someone who names you as their manager has completed the Claude Access – Artificial Intelligence Policy Acknowledgment. It opens a read-only list of your team’s acknowledgements — see AI Policy Acknowledgments (Managers). Nothing to approve.

Administrator tile

Only for portal administrators. It carries the configuration pages and is covered under Administrator Role.

ℹ️
If the portal holds more steps than you see tiles for, a note under the heading says how many are hidden and why. Hidden simply means you hold no approval access for that step.

📊 Overview

Where your role lands (the sidebar calls it Overview). Every tile and card is a shortcut, and the counts cover every request type your role can see — AI Policy acknowledgements included where you are allowed to see them.

⏰ Needs my action

Requests sitting at a step you can decide right now. This is your real queue.

🔄 In progress elsewhere

Pending, but at another step in the chain. Nothing for you to do yet.

✅ Approved

Finished the whole chain.

✖️ Rejected / Returned

Closed without approval.

Below the tiles: Pending Approvals and Recently Completed show the five newest of each, Needs Your Attention lists high-priority items waiting at your step, and Quick Actions jumps to the pages you use most. Click any request to open it.

⚠️
Administrators only: an amber note appears at the top of the dashboard when a step has no notification recipient. Requests reaching that step are escalated to the IT Service Desk until an address is set.

⏳ Needs my action, All Requests & Completed

Three lists over the same data, filtered differently. Each shows 50 rows a page with a type, stage and status column; every row opens the same detail drawer.
1

Needs my action

your queue

Only requests waiting at your step that you are allowed to decide. If this list is empty, nothing needs you.

2

All Requests

context

Every request whose workflow includes your step, whatever its status. Useful for “has this already been through us?”. Portal-wide grants see every request.

3

Completed

history

Approved, rejected and returned requests together — plus completed AI Policy acknowledgements for administrators and for the manager they name.

🔎

Search, filters & saved views

Search box

Plain text matches anywhere in the request — ID, requester, department, email, justification. Add a prefix to search one field only. Press / anywhere in the console to jump to the search box.

Prefixes
id:   type:   dept:   requester:   manager:   approver:   status:   stage:   submitted:   completed:
Example
type:oca status:pending dept:Finance   ·   requester:jdoe submitted:2026-09
Filter bar

Request type, status, stage, department and date range, combined with the search. Filters apply to the list you are on and the counts update as you go.

Saved views

Save view keeps the current search and filters under a name of your choice so you can return to it in one click. Views are stored in your browser, not shared.

Export CSV

Downloads the rows you are looking at, with the filters applied, for a spreadsheet or a report.

🔄
The list is a snapshot from when you loaded it. Use Refresh before a decision if the page has been open a while — and see Troubleshooting for what happens if someone else decided first.

🔍 Opening a Request

Click any row or tile. The detail panel slides in from the right.
A

Status

Current status, priority, and — while pending — which step it is waiting at.

B

Requester

Name, work email, department, job title, employee ID and direct manager, as captured at submission.

⚠️
An amber pill reading “no email — manager step uses the fallback recipient” means the requester typed their manager’s name without an address, so the Dept. Manager notification went to the fallback instead of their actual manager. Treat the manager approval on that request with extra care.
C

Request details

Every field the requester filled in for that form type, in the form’s own words. This is what you are reviewing.

D

Approval workflow

The full chain as a ladder: each step, who decided, when, and the comment they left. A filled dot is decided, a ring is the current step, a hollow ring has not been reached.

E

History

Every audited event on this request in order — submission, notifications, decisions, administrator edits — with timestamp and actor.

F

Word · PDF · Print

every drawer

Buttons in the drawer header download the full record — requester, every field, the workflow with decisions and comments, and the history — as a Word document or a PDF, or send it to the printer. AI Policy acknowledgement drawers export the same way. Each export is itself written to the audit trail.

📖
Read the workflow ladder before deciding. An earlier approver’s comment often answers the question you were about to ask the requester.

✅ Making a Decision

Three buttons at the foot of the detail panel, plus one comment box.
Read the request→ Write a comment→ Approve / Reject / Return one decision per step
🚨
A decision is final. There is no undo, and no way to re-open a request you have decided. If you are unsure, return it instead — or ask the requester before you click.
Comment required to reject or return

Optional when you approve, mandatory when you reject or return. It is shown to the requester in their email and on the workflow ladder to every other approver, so write it for them.

Good
Approved for the Madrid engagement only, 3–17 Oct. VPN + MFA required.
Good
Returned: name the client system and the data classification you need to reach.
✓

Approve

moves forward

Your step is recorded as approved and the request moves to the next step in the chain, whose recipient is emailed automatically. If yours was the last step, the request becomes Approved, the requester is told, and for access request types the IT Service Desk is emailed to action the grant.

✔️

Conditions belong in the comment. The portal has no partial approval — an approval with conditions written down is how a limit gets carried to the next approver and to IT.

✗

Reject

closes the request

Use it when the answer is no and no rewrite would change it. The request closes immediately — remaining steps are not consulted — and the requester is emailed with your step, your comment and the request ID to reference if they submit again.

↩

Return

closes the request

Use it when the request is reasonable but you cannot judge it as written — missing justification, wrong system named, no data classification. The requester is emailed your comment and asked to submit a new request that addresses it.

⚠️
Returned is a closed status, not a pause. The requester cannot edit and resubmit the same request; they file a new one. Say in your comment exactly what to change, so the second attempt is right.
✉️
All three outcomes email the requester automatically. You do not need to tell them separately.

📜 Audit Trail

Under Tools in the sidebar. One expandable record per request, plus a live stream of the newest events across everything you can see.

Click a request to expand its events: when, event, actor and note. Submissions, notifications, decisions and administrator edits all appear. An administrator edit is shown field by field as old → new. Open jumps to the full request.

🔎
This is the record to quote in a compliance review or when someone asks who approved something and when. It covers the requests your role can see.

🛡️ AI Policy Acknowledgments (Managers)

Standard Claude access is obtained by completing the Claude Access – Artificial Intelligence Policy Acknowledgment on the public portal. Nothing is approved — you are informed.
A

What you receive

When a person who names you as their manager completes the acknowledgement, you receive a copy of the confirmation email. IT is notified separately and provisions the Claude access.

B

Team acknowledgments

The Team acknowledgments tile on the role screen (and the Completed list) shows your team’s records: who signed, when, and against which policy version. Open a row for the full read-only record — details as signed, the statements accepted word for word, the signature — and download it as Word or PDF.

ℹ️
A team member who needs Claude but is not a full-time employee will not see the acknowledgement until an administrator adds them to the eligibility list. Send that request to IT with the person’s work email and the business reason.

ℹ️
Step names shown here are the current display names. A request keeps the chain it was submitted with, so changing a workflow in the portal code affects new requests only — renaming a step, by contrast, changes the name everywhere at once.

🏷️ Understanding Statuses

Four statuses you will see. Three of them are final.

Pending

Somewhere in the chain. The detail panel says which step. Only that step can act.

Approved

Every step approved. Final. The requester is told, and access request types also notify the IT Service Desk for fulfilment.

Rejected

Declined at a step. Final — remaining steps are skipped. The requester gets the step and the comment.

Returned

Sent back for rework, and also final: the requester submits a new request rather than editing this one.

🛡️ Who May Approve What

Three routes grant a step. All of them are deny-by-default.
  • 1The step’s security group. Membership of a verified AD security group grants only the steps and request types selected in its enabled Approval Access policy. This is the route to use for a standing team.
  • 2A named grant. A super-admin grants the person selected departments and request types under Approval Access → App-only override. The employee’s HR Department does not determine this access. View and approval rights are separate; grants can expire.
  • 3Being the manager on the request. The Dept. Manager step is granted per request to whoever the request names as the requester’s manager. No group membership is needed, and it gives no rights on any other request.
🚫
You cannot decide your own request, by any route, including as an administrator. The service refuses it.
ℹ️
Seeing a request and deciding it are separate rights. A portal-wide grant may let someone read every request while deciding none — the detail panel says so in place of the decision buttons.

✉️ Which Emails Go Out

Nobody has to send anything by hand.
1

On submission

The requester gets a confirmation with their request ID. The first step’s recipient gets the “approval needed” email.

2

On approval, mid-chain

The next step’s recipient is notified. For the Dept. Manager step that is the requester’s own manager when the request carries their address, and the configured fallback when it does not.

3

On a final outcome

Approved, Rejected and Returned all email the requester, naming the deciding step and quoting the comment. Approved access requests also email the IT Service Desk for fulfilment.

⚠️
A step with no recipient does not fail silently. The request is escalated to the IT Service Desk address and a notification.unroutable event is written to the audit trail. Administrators also see an amber warning on the dashboard until the address is set.

⚙️ Administrator Role

Choose the Administrator tile on the role screen. The sidebar gains an Administration section.
🚨
Administrator is configuration, not a licence to decide. It does not grant approval rights: an administrator can only decide a request whose current step they hold through one of the three routes above.

Every setting on a step applies to every request type and department that uses that step. The one exception is Dept. Manager, which routes per request.

🧩 Manage Roles

The map of the whole workflow, and where a step gets renamed.
The step table

One row per step: its display name, its internal key and policy ownership, its description, which request types use it, who may approve, and who is notified. The Approvers and Recipient buttons jump to the page that changes each.

Rename next to the step name

Changes the display name everywhere at once — role tiles, queues, the workflow ladder, the request-type chips on the main portal and the notification emails. The internal key and access policies never change, so nobody’s access is affected.

To reset
Clear the field and save — the built-in name comes back.
✔️

Hard-reload the main portal (Cmd/Ctrl+Shift+R) after renaming if a browser still shows the old name from cache.

Workflows by request type

Administrators can reorder, add or remove steps and hide request types. Changes apply to new requests; existing requests retain their saved chain.

📧 Notification Emails

Who is emailed at each step, and what that email says.
Recipient name and email

One address per step, shared by every request type that uses it. Recipient name is only the greeting. Save applies immediately. Clearing the address unsets the step — requests reaching it escalate to the Service Desk.

✔️

A shared team mailbox or distribution list survives holidays and staff changes; a single person’s address does not.

Edit email

Changes the subject and the opening wording for that step’s “approval needed” email, with a Preview tab. Leave a field blank to keep the built-in template; a step with custom wording is marked Custom. The request-details table, the action button and the footer are always built by the portal service.

Fixed recipient — IT Service Desk

Read-only here. It receives approved access requests for fulfilment and any step that has no recipient. It is set in the service environment, so changing it needs a service restart.

⚠️
Who is notified and who may approve are configured separately, and the portal does not warn when they disagree. After changing one, check the other on Manage Roles.

🔐 Approval Access

Super-admin only. Everyone else sees a “Super-admin only” notice.

Policies are the standing grants. Each names a principal — one or more AD groups, or one person — and says which steps it can see and which of those it can decide. A policy can be edited, disabled without deleting, or deleted. A decide-everything policy is flagged high privilege.

Tier 2

Shown on its own card: the portal-wide policy for the tier2 group. Its enabled policy determines its scope; Tier 2 membership alone does not grant approval decisions.

App-only overrides

Use App-only override to select a person, departments, request types, view/approve rights, optional expiration and a reason. Edit or disable that person’s policy in the same list. Person blocks take precedence over ordinary grants in the selected scope.

Check and manage department access

In Administrator → Approval Access, enter a company email and choose Check access to see live directory membership, effective resource scope, grant sources and portal-entry blocks. Use Refresh AD access after directory changes.

Manage AD security groups shows direct members of mapped groups. Adding or removing a member requires a reason and a review of any other application rights that group carries. Existing department groups also grant AI Usage Credits access; choose an app-only override when that extra access is unwanted. Nested memberships are not used.

The shared Portal entry rule allows entry, configuration administration, or an explicit block. Entry and configuration rights do not grant approval decisions. Corporate sign-in, disabled-account checks and additional site restrictions remain enforced. Superadmin recovery oversight is separate from decision rights.

CorpIT Access Control links to this owner screen. Subadmin site restrictions apply only to protected approver routes; requester forms remain public. Notification recipients never grant approval rights.

📝 Site Content

The wording on the public portal and in the emails.

A tabbed editor for the main portal’s headings, the request-type names and descriptions, the confirmation page and the sentences used in notification emails. Edits are staged, then published together after a review dialog that shows every old → new change. Clearing a field restores the built-in default.

⚠️
Placeholders such as {id} and {type} only work where the portal offers them for that sentence. Anything else is left as literal text. This guide and the Requestor Guide are static pages and are not edited here.

📄 Form Policies (Site Content tab)

One AI Policy document serves both the AI Use Case form and the AI Policy Acknowledgement. Everything the employee reads on the acknowledgement page is set here.
1

AI Policy — document

Upload the PDF and Publish new version. Each publish creates a new version; earlier versions are kept for the records that were signed against them. Release this page to employees must be ticked for the acknowledgement to be available on the portal. Employees see the document name, not the version number; the version is stamped on every record and shown in the audit trail and the admin console.

2

Introduction & acknowledgment statements

Introduction is the blue box above the checkboxes. Acknowledgment statements are the checkboxes themselves — one required checkbox per line. Start a line with - for an indented checkbox under the line above it, and with * for a plain bullet inside that item. <strong>…</strong> bolds a lead-in. Do not type ☐ — the page draws the boxes. Leave Key requirements empty unless a separate highlighted list is wanted.

Example
I have read and understand the AI Policy… I further understand the key highlights… - <strong>Protect restricted data.</strong> Do not enter… - <strong>New AI Use Case Intake Form Required…</strong> If your use expands to: * Development of AI agents… * Connectivity to client systems…

Editing the statements changes what new signers see; it does not by itself ask existing signers to re-acknowledge. When Legal changes the provisions materially, publish a new document version as well — everyone who signed against the earlier version is then asked to acknowledge again on their next visit.

3

Notifications & provisioning

Admin / IT notification recipients is the list that provisions Claude — it receives a notice per acknowledgement or a daily summary. Manager copy sends the employee’s manager a copy of the confirmation. Claude provisioning lead time is the number of business days quoted to the employee and in the IT notice (default 2).

⚠️
Edits here are staged like any other Site Content change: click Publish changes at the bottom, then hard-reload the portal page to check the result.

🛡️ AI Policy Acknowledgments (Administration)

Every acknowledgement recorded, who may complete one, and the notices that went out.
A

Records

One row per acknowledgement: person, department, manager, policy version, how they reviewed the policy, eligible via (HR roster or administrator grant) and when. Search, filter by version or date, open the full read-only record, download Word / PDF, or export the list as CSV or a ZIP of records for a compliance review. Counts at the top show eligible people and how many have not yet acknowledged.

B

Eligibility

Full-time employees (HR pay class) are eligible automatically. The list adds exceptions: Allow lets a contractor or other worker type complete the acknowledgement; Block hides it from someone who would otherwise be eligible. Enter the work email, a reason, and tick Notify to email the person that the acknowledgement is now available (a resend button is on each row). Removing an entry is audited; earlier acknowledgements are kept.

📌

Rule: eligible = (HR full-time or active Allow) and not active Block. A blocked or ineligible person who tries to submit is refused and the attempt is audited.

C

What the audit trail shows

Each record carries the employee confirmation, the manager copy and the IT notice as separate events (sent or failed, with the address), plus any export, refusal or eligibility change. Quote this when someone asks whether a person was notified.

▣ Archiving

Move finished or test records out of the working views. Nothing is deleted — archived items keep their history and exports and can be brought back.
A

What archiving does

An archived request leaves Overview counts, Needs my action, All requests, Completed and the request-type tiles. It stays in the database and the Audit trail, its Word / PDF / Print exports still work, and the requester’s Track Your Request shows it with an “archived” note. Every archive and un-archive is written to the audit trail with the reason you give.

B

How to archive

One record: open it and click ▣ Archive in the drawer header, next to Word / PDF / Print. Several: in All requests or Completed, tick the boxes in the first column and click Archive selected…. A reason is required either way.

ℹ️
Approvers archive completed requests (approved, rejected, returned) in their own scope. A request that is still pending can only be archived by an administrator — if it is at your step, use Reject or Return instead. While a pending request is archived, nobody can decide it and no notifications go out.
C

Finding archived records

Archived in the sidebar (under Requests) lists everything archived that your role can see: who archived it, when and why. Search, open the full drawer, download the record, or export the list as CSV. Administrators can tick rows and Un-archive them, which restores the previous state.

D

Administrators — Archive by date

Administration → Archive: archive everything submitted before a date (requests, acknowledgments or both), with a preview count first and a typed ARCHIVE confirmation. Used for the go-live clean-up of test data. Archived acknowledgments are treated as not signed: the person completes the real acknowledgement afresh.

◈ AI Governance (InfoSec, Legal, administrators)

The AI application inventory, risk assessments, usage evidence and executive reports live in the AI Governance Workspace on CorpIT. Sidebar → AI Governance ↗ opens it in a new tab. Other roles do not see the link.
A

Opening the workspace

Click AI Governance in the sidebar, then Open AI Governance Workspace. CorpIT asks for its own sign-in the first time. The jump tiles open a specific section: AI Inventory, Risk Assessments, Governance Queue, Coverage Gaps, Executive Reports.

B

How approved intake reaches it

Every approved AI Use Case Intake request is pulled into AI Governance on a schedule by CorpIT (one-way, read-only). Reopened or archived requests are marked no longer approved there. Approval of a use case here and approval of an application there remain separate decisions.

C

Administrators: the sync token

On the AI Governance page, administrators see Integration. Generate token shows a service token once; hand it to the CorpIT owner for the worker. Revoke stops the feed immediately. Every feed read and token change is written to the Audit trail.

🔧 Troubleshooting

What the console is telling you, and what to do about it.
“This request changed since you loaded it — refreshed”

Someone decided, or an administrator edited the request, after your page loaded. Nothing of yours was saved. The panel reloads with the current version — read it again before deciding.

“It is not this step’s turn”

The request has moved on. Refresh; if it is now at your step it will be back in Pending Approvals.

No decision buttons on a pending request

The panel says why: it is waiting at another step, or you hold view rights but not decide rights for the current step, or it is your own request.

“You are not an assigned approver for this step”

Your grant does not cover the step the request is actually at. A super-admin can check your effective access and grant the required department/resource under Approval Access.

A role tile you expected is missing

You hold no approval access for that step. The note under the heading counts the hidden roles.

An approver says they were never emailed

Open the request’s History, or the Audit Trail, and look for the notification event for that step. A notification.unroutable event means the step had no recipient and the mail went to the Service Desk instead.

⚡ Tips for Approvers

  • 1Work from Pending Approvals, not email. The queue is the truth; an email can be forwarded, filtered or stale.
  • 2Read the ladder first. Earlier approvers’ comments usually contain the context you are missing.
  • 3Write the comment before you click. It is the only thing the requester receives, and the only thing later approvers see from you.
  • 4Return rather than guess. A returned request with a specific instruction costs the requester one form; a wrong approval costs an access review.
  • 5Put conditions in writing. Dates, systems, IP restrictions, expiry — the comment is what IT reads when they action the grant.
  • 6Check the manager pill on Dept. Manager approvals. A missing manager email means the notification went to the fallback address, not the requester’s manager.
  • 7Refresh before deciding on a page you have had open a while, especially when a step has several approvers.
  • 8Prefer groups to named grants. Group membership follows joiners and leavers; a named grant has to be remembered and removed by hand.
